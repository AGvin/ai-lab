# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Present this scenario for one professional in legal, accounting, healthcare, consulting, or another context where confidential, privileged, regulated, or otherwise sensitive data materially constrains model access and review.
- Preserve a contracted enterprise service as a candidate only after retention, region/residency, access, audit, training-use, subprocessors, and other applicable contractual/data terms are approved for the exact data class.
- Preserve a private managed API/project or private deployment only when the complete provider/cloud/identity architecture is approved; state explicitly that data still leaves the endpoint owner unless the architecture proves otherwise.
- Preserve Gemma 4 E2B/E4B Instruct as compact local multimodal candidates for bounded text/image/document/short-audio tasks and Phi-4 Mini Instruct, Qwen3 8B, or Qwen3 14B as local text candidates when exact hardware/runtime quality is sufficient.
- Bind local-route claims to exact model artifact, runtime, storage, logs, deletion controls, context, modality limits, and measured quality. Local processing does not by itself establish legal/compliance suitability or adequate domain accuracy.
- Treat legacy 16–32 GB RAM and measured 24 GB GPU examples only as route-planning context, not guaranteed fit or sufficient quality evidence.
- Require qualified human review or prohibit model use when the local/hosted quality ceiling, domain accuracy, OCR/audio limitations, evidence quality, or failure severity makes the task unsuitable for automated output.
- Do not recommend a free consumer service, aggregator, coding client, or routing intermediary merely for convenience. Confirm the complete provider/subprocessor chain and applicable legal/contractual basis first.
- Keep organization-wide compliance architecture, policy, procurement, and platform governance outside this individual scenario; link the applicable organization route when those controls determine what the professional may use.
- Compare approved enterprise/private/local routes by accepted-result quality, review burden, operational cost, and data risk rather than treating privacy as the only decision axis.

## Validation

- Convenience or provider marketing is not treated as approval for sensitive data.
- Complete provider/client/subprocessor chain and applicable data terms are considered before external processing.
- Local processing is not presented as sufficient compliance, legal, or quality evidence by itself.
- Material failure severity and qualified-review requirements can make a model route unsuitable even when technically available.
