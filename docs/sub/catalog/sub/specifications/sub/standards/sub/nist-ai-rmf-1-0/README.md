# NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0

The **NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0**, also cited as **NIST AI 100-1**, is a [voluntary AI risk-management framework published on January 26, 2023](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10) by the [U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology](../../../../../producers/sub/n/sub/nist/).

## Scope and lifecycle

The framework supports organizational management of risks from AI systems across design, development, deployment, evaluation and use. The [Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/) is organized into four functions: **Govern, Map, Measure, Manage**. They express outcomes and practices intended for continuous risk management, not a mandatory sequential checklist. Adopting the framework is not itself certification, proof of conformity with ISO/IEC 42001, legal compliance or validation that a model is safe.

**Version status:** NIST's [current framework hub](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) says **AI RMF 1.0 is being revised** as of 2026. This record represents the published 2023 edition, not a future or preview replacement. Check the authoritative NIST page for any subsequently completed revision before applying it as a current regulatory or procurement reference.

## Companions and boundaries

The separate [2024 Generative AI Profile, NIST AI 600-1](../nist-ai-600-1-generative-ai-profile/), focuses the framework on generative-model risks; it is not a new 1.0 edition. The [NIST AI RMF Playbook](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/nist-ai-rmf-playbook) offers evolving, voluntary implementation suggestions. The Playbook and GenAI Profile are not a substitute for context-specific technical controls, original AI model provenance, security testing or laws applicable to the actual deployed system.

NIST publishes risk-management guidance rather than a trained model, commercial API, private governance platform or certification service. Control selection, audit evidence, regional laws and current NIST revisions require independent review before asserting compliance.
