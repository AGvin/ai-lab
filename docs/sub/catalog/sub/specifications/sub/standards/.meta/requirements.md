# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Present Standards as the specification group for identifiable formal normative artifacts whose upstream identity is a standard and whose contract centers on requirements, controls, interoperability, or conformance.
- Materialize this group only through concrete source-backed standards with durable reader value; do not create taxonomy-only child placeholders for logical completeness.
- Preserve each standard's authoritative identity, governance/maintainer, maturity, released revision, normative requirements, extension/profile model, and conformance semantics only to the depth supported by current upstream sources.
- Distinguish standards from protocols, formats, schemas, guidance frameworks, threat lists, implementation libraries, and proposals according to the artifact's canonical upstream identity and role.
- Do not promote a draft, proposal, preview, guidance document, framework, checklist, or community taxonomy into a ratified/released standard merely because it is authoritative or widely cited.
- Keep product implementations, deployment tutorials, and dated evaluation evidence with their proper catalog, learning, concept, or decision-support owners.

## Validation

- Every concrete standard node cites authoritative/upstream evidence for both identity and maturity/status.
- Standard placement reflects the artifact's own canonical identity and normative role rather than a locally invented classification label.
- Version-sensitive requirements and conformance behavior are not presented as timeless.
- Current navigation exposes only materialized standards and does not imply that unmaterialized logical candidates are absent from the selected architecture.
