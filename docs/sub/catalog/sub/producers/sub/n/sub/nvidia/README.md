# NVIDIA

NVIDIA is the producer organization represented across hardware, software, models, services, Agent Skills, and research artifacts in this catalog. Its legal material identifies NVIDIA Corporation. [Legal information](https://www.nvidia.com/en-in/drivers/legalfo/).

## Scope and operating boundaries

An NVIDIA publication or repository namespace establishes the stated provenance, not one license, support contract, or deployment policy for the entire portfolio. Open-source projects, commercial software, downloadable models, and cloud services retain their own terms and operators. [Open-source scope](https://opensource.nvidia.com/llm-info/); [Agreements](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/agreements/).

Joint projects retain their own governance. NVIDIA is one founding organization of llm-d, whose project is hosted in the CNCF Sandbox; that participation does not establish exclusive ownership or maintenance. [llm-d repository](https://github.com/llm-d/llm-d); [CNCF project](https://www.cncf.io/projects/llm-d/).

The NVIDIA Groq hardware lineage uses licensed Groq technology. The published non-exclusive agreement and NVIDIA product announcement do not establish ownership of Groq or operation of GroqCloud. [Groq agreement](https://groq.com/newsroom/groq-and-nvidia-enter-non-exclusive-inference-technology-licensing-agreement-to-accelerate-ai-inference-at-global-scale).

The Gretel relation records an acquired business lineage. Acquisition provenance does not establish continued availability of its former hosted platform. [NVIDIA author biography](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/author/alexwatson/).

Cloud infrastructure location and product provenance also leave room for different operators: Brev provisions resources across cloud providers. Customer accounts, service agreements, hardware integration, and research-use limits must be assessed for the chosen product or deployment. [Brev documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/brev/getting-started/overview).
