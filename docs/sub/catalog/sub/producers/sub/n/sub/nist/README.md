# National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

The [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://www.nist.gov/about-nist) is a **non-regulatory U.S. federal agency within the Department of Commerce**, established in **1901**. Its scientific measurement, standards and research work includes [NIST AI Risk Management Framework guidance](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework). It is not a commercial model API provider or an AI system certification vendor.

## AI guidance provenance

NIST's published AI guidance includes two independently identifiable documents relevant to this catalog:

- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0](../../../../../specifications/sub/standards/sub/nist-ai-rmf-1-0/) — **NIST AI 100-1**, published January 26, 2023 as voluntary, general-purpose risk management guidance.
- [NIST AI 600-1 Generative AI Profile](../../../../../specifications/sub/standards/sub/nist-ai-600-1-generative-ai-profile/) — separately published July 26, 2024, applying the framework to generative-AI-specific risks.

Both are **voluntary** guidance artifacts. They are not certified model safety assessments, formal ISO/IEC 42001 certificates, current laws, or independent proof of a vendor's reliability. A published NIST guidance document does not transfer authorship of any private AI model or system to NIST.

## Currentness and public-sector boundary

NIST's [current AI RMF hub](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) states that AI RMF 1.0 is being revised. This does not by itself create a final replacement for the official 2023 document or update the separate 2024 profile. Check actual publication statuses and applicable laws when selecting controls, carrying out regulatory analyses or preparing audit evidence. The agency's federal legal position is public-sector provenance, not a shareholder/ultimate beneficial ownership certification for private providers.
