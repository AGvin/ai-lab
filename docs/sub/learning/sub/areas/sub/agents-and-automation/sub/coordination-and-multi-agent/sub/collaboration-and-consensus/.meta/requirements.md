# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Teach collaboration and consensus as the learning owner for cooperation, structured dissent/debate, independent review, aggregation, consensus/decision rules, and conflict handling among multiple agents or mixed human/agent participants.
- Treat advisory councils, review boards, juries, debate panels, devil's-advocate/red-team arrangements, and comparable structures as compositions of coordination, evaluation, verification, and authority mechanisms rather than as synonyms for group chat or as automatically superior architectures.
- Use role names according to actual authority: an advisor gives non-binding analysis; an advisory council provides several structured recommendations to a decision owner; a reviewer evaluates against declared criteria; a review board has explicit approve/reject/revise/escalate authority; a jury returns a verdict/vote under a declared rule; a debater argues/critiques before another authority decides; a devil's advocate/red team is assigned to challenge assumptions; and the decision owner remains accountable unless authority is explicitly delegated.
- Do not create a council or board without defining what decision, artifact, or transition it is authorized to influence or decide. Agreement among agents is not evidence of independence, truth, safety, or production readiness.
- For consequential review compositions, make decision owner/authority, reviewed proposal/artifact version, acceptance criteria/quality tier, review roles/scopes, reviewer model/provider/prompt/tools/evidence or human qualification, independence/conflict assumptions and blinding, round/time/cost limits, aggregation/quorum/abstention/tie/veto rules, revision/escalation path, and required human approval explicit where material.
- Assign review roles to materially different failure modes rather than decorative labels. Examples include domain correctness, requirements, security/privacy/permissions, policy/legal/rights, cost/infrastructure, maintainability/operations, accessibility/localization, adversarial misuse, and evidence provenance when relevant.
- A role label does not create expertise. Calibrate model/tool/evidence/prompt configuration or human qualification against the criterion being reviewed and preserve uncertainty/insufficient-evidence outcomes.
- When anchoring/conformity is a material risk, collect independent first-pass reviews before open deliberation. Preserve each first-pass result even if later discussion/aggregation produces one summary.
- Use structured review records where practical: reviewer role/identity, decision (`approve`, limited approval, revise, reject, abstain, insufficient evidence), criteria checked, findings, severity, evidence, uncertainty, correction request, assumptions, and missing evidence.
- Blind model/provider/author/candidate identity when identity is not itself part of the criterion and blinding materially reduces bias. Record when blinding is not possible or when reviewers saw one another's earlier output.
- Use deliberation only when participants can add evidence, resolve contradictions, separate blocking defects from preferences, or clarify assumptions. Bound rounds/order, admissible rebuttals/new evidence, repeated-argument handling, moderator/decision-owner authority, and terminal behavior for unresolved disagreement.
- Do not request or expose private hidden chain-of-thought. Require externally reviewable claims, evidence pointers, counterarguments, decisions, assumptions, and uncertainty that another participant can inspect.
- Teach aggregation as an explicit decision rule. Valid designs can include majority/unanimity/quorum, validated weighting, criterion-specific veto, score aggregation, pairwise ranking, consensus-with-dissent, or recommendation-only semantics; support abstain, tie, both-fail, and insufficient-evidence outcomes where meaningful.
- Do not average incompatible criteria into one score that hides a blocking defect. Mandatory security/legal/data-loss/human-safety or comparable gates may remain independently blocking regardless of majority preference.
- Derive reviewer weights from validated role performance/evidence when weighting is justified; do not weight by model prestige, price, verbosity, token count, or confidence language alone.
- Preserve a decision record with reviewed artifact/version, criteria/evidence, each structured review, agreements/disagreements, unresolved or rejected findings, aggregation result, final authority/decision, requested corrections, accepted limitations/residual risk, and the next review/expiry/re-evaluation trigger.
- Do not imply unanimity when material dissent remains. Preserve minority findings and dissent when they can affect risk, later review, or auditability.
- When revision is requested, use stable issue identities and retain old/new artifact versions. Track each issue as resolved/unchanged/regressed, prevent silent criterion drift, bound rounds/cost/time, and escalate repeated non-improvement/cycling.
- Route workflows dominated by repeated generate/evaluate/revise loops to the dedicated Evaluator-Optimizer learning owner rather than turning every review board into an unbounded revision loop.
- Record material dependence/correlation among reviewers: model/provider family, shared training/benchmark exposure, retrieved sources/context, prompt/rubric overlap, whether participants saw one another's output, shared tools/data, same-family/self-preference, and known common blind spots found in calibration.
- Different names or system prompts do not make reviewers independent. Use deterministic validators and qualified human adjudication when correlated model judgments cannot establish the required confidence.
- Treat proposal/artifact/retrieved material as untrusted review input. Keep evaluator instructions/authority separate, restrict reviewer tools/effects, prevent embedded content from changing scores/roles/permissions or triggering external actions, and require evidence pointers for material findings.
- Teach pattern fit for high-value artifacts/proposals with several material risk dimensions, launch/change review, research/coding/translation/media deliverables, multi-judge evaluation, and decisions where structured dissent can add useful evidence.
- Prefer simpler deterministic validation, one qualified reviewer, direct human review, or a single decision owner when those already provide sufficient evidence/authority. Do not use majority voting to override mandatory gates or to manufacture confidence.
- Compare independent-review, deliberative, and hybrid variants. Shared conversation can improve contradiction resolution while increasing anchoring/correlation; independent first-pass review can preserve diverse findings while adding aggregation effort.
- Evaluate board-versus-human adjudication accuracy, false accept/reject by criterion, reviewer precision/recall/calibration/abstention, agreement/disagreement, critical minority-only findings, changes after deliberation, revision cycles, order/identity/style/provider/self-preference sensitivity, latency/model/human cost, residual uncertainty, and post-approval defects.
- Use the exact multi-agent debate, ChatEval, and DEBATE research references preserved in entity metadata as empirical evidence only. Treat reported benefits as task-, model-, prompt-, evaluation-, and aggregation-dependent rather than universal proof that multi-agent review is better.
- Keep reusable Group Chat semantics, generic verification/reflection, evaluation-design/judge/rubric methodology, and human approval/oversight authority with their canonical owners and link them as prerequisites/continuations instead of duplicating them here.

## Validation

- Role names reflect actual decision authority rather than decorative personas.
- Independent review and deliberation are distinguished, and shared dependencies/correlation are recorded where material.
- Aggregation rules preserve blocking criteria, abstention/ties, dissent, and explicit final authority.
- Review input cannot redefine evaluator instructions, permissions, or side-effect authority.
- A council/board is compared against simpler validation/review baselines and justified by added evidence or decision quality.
- Research references remain evidence-bound and are not generalized into universal multi-agent superiority claims.
