# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Teach Text to Speech as synthesizing spoken audio from text while distinguishing it from general audio generation, voice conversion, and voice cloning.
- Use accessibility, narration, announcements, prototypes, voice interfaces, localization, and assistive communication as application examples rather than task-definition requirements.
- Explain that pronunciation dictionaries, phonetic controls, SSML-like markup, prosody controls, or other runtime features can help with names, abbreviations, numbers, dates, and domain vocabulary, but their syntax and behavior are implementation-specific.
- Review pronunciation and intended meaning for critical information instead of assuming natural-sounding speech is correct.
- For cloned or otherwise identifiable voices, require appropriate permission and protect reference recordings and speaker-related data according to applicable privacy, licensing, disclosure, impersonation, fraud-prevention, platform, and publication rules.
- Treat synthetic-speech disclosure as context-dependent governance rather than a model-mechanics property.

## Validation

- Naturalness is not equated with semantic or pronunciation correctness.
- Concrete control syntax is not generalized across runtimes.
- Identifiable voice use keeps explicit consent and disclosure boundaries.
