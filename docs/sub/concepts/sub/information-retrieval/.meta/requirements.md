# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Use the reader-facing title `Information Retrieval (IR)`.
- Define information retrieval as the process and study of representing, indexing, searching, ranking, and returning information items that are relevant to an information need from a collection or accessible information source.
- Distinguish retrieval from generation. A retrieval system selects or ranks existing information items or passages; a generative model may subsequently use retrieved material, but generation is not part of the generic retrieval definition.
- Present lexical, semantic/vector, hybrid, indexing/chunking, reranking, and filtering as distinct selected concerns within the retrieval domain rather than interchangeable implementations of one operation.
- Explain that relevance is query/task/user-context dependent and is evaluated rather than guaranteed by a matching or ranking method alone.
- Distinguish retrieval methods from concrete search engines, vector databases, embedding models, hosted services, and application-specific RAG pipelines; those concrete entities or larger architectures retain their applicable canonical owners.
- Render the standard direct-child navigation from the validated materialized child set when reader-facing rendering is activated.
- Keep concrete indexes, corpus facts, benchmark results, product compatibility, operational configuration, and model-selection recommendations with their applicable catalog, evidence, engineering, or decision owners.
- Use the canonical entity references as research inputs for stable information-retrieval terminology when reader-facing rendering is activated.

## Validation

- The page does not equate information retrieval with RAG, vector search, semantic search, or web search alone.
- Retrieval is distinguished from generation and from concrete storage/search products.
- Relevance is not treated as guaranteed by one similarity score or ranking function.
- Direct-child navigation contains only currently materialized direct children.
