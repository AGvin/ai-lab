# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Use the reader-facing title `Frontier Models`.
- Define a frontier model as a model whose demonstrated capabilities place it at or near the leading capability boundary for a stated scope and time; present frontier status as relative and time-sensitive rather than as a permanent intrinsic class.
- Acknowledge that authoritative sources also use broader `frontier AI` terminology for particularly capable general-purpose AI systems; keep this node's model-level meaning explicit when terminology could otherwise include complete systems or scaffolds.
- When applying frontier status to a concrete model, require the exact model/version or service snapshot, the capability scope being assessed, the supporting evidence, and the relevant verification date.
- Treat narrow benchmark or task evidence as support only for the capability scope it actually measures; do not generalize one leading result into universal frontier status without broader evidence.
- Keep frontier status independent from language-model scale, architecture, deployment mode, access model, ecosystem maturity, safety, reliability, cost efficiency, and suitability for a specific workflow.
- Treat provider claims as potentially useful evidence but not as sufficient independent proof when the frontier distinction materially affects an evaluation or decision.
- Distinguish generic frontier-model terminology from jurisdiction-specific legal or regulatory classifications. In particular, do not treat the EU `general-purpose AI model with systemic risk` criteria or its training-compute presumption as a universal definition of `frontier model`.
- Keep named lists or rankings of currently frontier models, concrete benchmark results, compact comparison-status vocabularies, and model-selection consequences with their evidence, evaluation, or decision owners rather than hard-coding them into this concept.
- Use the canonical entity references as research inputs for frontier terminology, capability-evidence boundaries, and regulatory distinctions when reader-facing rendering is activated.

## Validation

- The page does not define `frontier` as a synonym for newest, largest, most expensive, most popular, or most heavily trained.
- The page does not preserve a frontier label indefinitely without a stated evidence scope and temporal boundary.
- The page does not infer safety, reliability, low hallucination rate, tool-use reliability, or workflow suitability from frontier capability alone.
- A task- or benchmark-specific result is not presented as proof of leadership across unrelated capability domains.
- Regulatory thresholds or legal classifications are clearly scoped to their jurisdictional purpose and are not substituted for the general concept definition.
- The page contains no mutable list of named current frontier models and does not duplicate comparison-table or model-selection workflow material from the legacy source.
