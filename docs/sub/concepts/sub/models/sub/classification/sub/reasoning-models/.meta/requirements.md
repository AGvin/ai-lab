# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Use the reader-facing title `Reasoning Models` and introduce `reasoning-oriented models` / `thinking models` as common overlapping ecosystem terms.
- Define this node as a practical model/configuration classification for systems whose training, inference design, or exposed operating mode is intentionally oriented toward allocating and using additional deliberative/test-time computation to improve performance on difficult multi-step, search, planning, verification, coding, mathematical, analytical, or constraint-heavy tasks before committing to a final result.
- Do not present `reasoning model` as one universal architecture type or a strict binary intrinsic property. The same model family can expose lower/higher reasoning effort, adaptive thinking, reasoning-off/standard modes, or provider-specific configurations; classification therefore depends on the concrete model/version/mode and intended operating behavior.
- Distinguish reasoning-oriented classification from generic reasoning capability. Many ordinary language/multimodal models can solve some multi-step tasks, while a reasoning-oriented model/mode is specifically designed, trained, or operated to invest additional inference work or use dedicated reasoning procedures for harder problems.
- Distinguish reasoning models from `models/behavior-and-failure-modes/reasoning/` and `chain-of-thought/`. This node classifies models/configurations; the behavior node owns reasoning phenomena/failure modes, and chain of thought owns one textual/intermediate-reasoning technique rather than the complete model class.
- Do not require visible chain-of-thought. Deliberative computation can be hidden, summarized, latent, recurrent, search-based, verifier-guided, tool-assisted, multi-sample, or otherwise implemented; user-visible reasoning text is neither necessary nor sufficient for the classification.
- Distinguish model-side reasoning from external inference orchestration. Sampling many answers, tree/search procedures, verifier/reranker loops, agent planning, tool workflows, or self-consistency wrappers can add test-time compute around a base model; they do not automatically turn the underlying base-model identity into a reasoning model even when the resulting inference system reasons more effectively.
- Explain test-time/inference-time compute as a broad family rather than only `reasoning tokens`. Additional work can involve longer sequential deliberation, adaptive depth, latent recurrent computation, generating multiple candidates, search over partial solutions, verification/critique, tool interaction, or combinations depending on the system.
- Treat provider concepts such as `reasoning_effort`, `thinking_level`, `thinking_budget`, adaptive thinking, pro/reasoning modes, or hidden reasoning tokens as concrete current interface/model facts rather than universal reasoning-model properties. Keep exact support/value ranges with the applicable catalog/provider/model owners.
- Distinguish configurable reasoning budget from context window and visible output length. A model can spend substantial internal/inference work while returning a concise answer; conversely, a long verbose answer is not proof that additional reasoning compute was used effectively.
- Do not equate internal reasoning-token count or generated trace length with reasoning quality. Longer deliberation can improve some problems, waste compute, repeat errors, pursue bad premises, or degrade performance depending on the task/model/procedure.
- Explain compute scaling as task-dependent. More inference-time computation can improve success on sufficiently difficult problems but gains can saturate, vary by problem difficulty, depend on verifier/search quality, and fail to compensate for missing knowledge, wrong assumptions, poor tools, or inadequate model capability.
- Distinguish reasoning effort from factual grounding/current knowledge. Additional deliberation cannot create authoritative external facts that are absent or outdated; retrieval/tools/source verification remain separate evidence mechanisms.
- Distinguish reasoning from correctness, calibration, safety, and authorization. A reasoning-oriented model can produce coherent but wrong arguments, overconfident answers, unsafe plans, or unauthorized actions; deterministic validation, permissions, and human oversight remain required where the application demands them.
- Treat reasoning traces/explanations as evidence artifacts cautiously. A visible rationale, reasoning summary, or step-by-step explanation can support review but is not guaranteed to expose the model's complete internal causal process and must not be treated as an audit log by default.
- Explain training approaches only at the classification boundary. Reasoning-oriented behavior can arise from pretraining, supervised reasoning traces, reinforcement/preference optimization, verifiable rewards, distillation, curriculum/data design, architectural recurrence, inference-time search, or combinations; detailed training-method semantics remain with `training-and-adaptation/` or other applicable owners.
- Explain that model labels are ecosystem-relative and time-sensitive. Providers/researchers may market a model as `reasoning`, `thinking`, `deliberative`, or general-purpose with reasoning modes; canonical classification should rely on documented behavior/interface/training/inference evidence rather than the marketing label alone.
- Require evaluation at a stated inference protocol and budget. Benchmark comparisons should identify the model/version, reasoning/thinking mode or effort, sampling/search/verification procedure, tool access, latency/token/compute budget, and pass/aggregation protocol when these materially affect results.
- Do not compare a single low-effort run against a multi-sample/search/high-effort system as though only base-model identity changed; reasoning-system evaluation must account for the actual inference procedure and resource budget.
- Explain workload fit as empirical. Reasoning-oriented operation is often most useful for hard, decomposable, constraint-rich, verifiable, or high-value tasks; simple extraction, classification, deterministic transformations, or latency-sensitive requests may gain little and can be better served by lower-effort/smaller/deterministic paths.
- Keep model-routing and cost/latency recommendations with their selected decision/engineering owners. This concept can explain qualitative trade-offs but must not become a current provider/model recommendation table.
- Keep concrete reasoning-model names/versions, provider parameters/defaults, hidden-token accounting, prices, latency, benchmark scores, current capability claims, and task-routing recommendations with their applicable catalog/evidence/decision owners.
- Use the canonical entity references as research inputs for configurable/adaptive reasoning modes and test-time-compute mechanisms when reader-facing rendering is activated.

## Validation

- `Reasoning model` is not presented as one architecture, a permanent binary property, or a provider marketing label accepted without evidence.
- Visible chain-of-thought, verbosity, reasoning-token count, or trace length is not treated as proof of reasoning quality or classification by itself.
- Model-side reasoning is distinguished from external search/sampling/verifier/agent orchestration.
- More inference-time compute is not claimed to guarantee monotonic quality, factual correctness, calibration, safety, or authorization.
- Internal/visible reasoning is not treated as an authoritative audit trail.
- Benchmark claims are scoped to inference protocol and compute/effort conditions when relevant.
- Concrete model/provider parameters, prices, benchmark results, and routing recommendations remain outside the reusable classification concept.
