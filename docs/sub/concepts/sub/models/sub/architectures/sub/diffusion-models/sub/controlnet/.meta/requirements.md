# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Use the reader-facing title `ControlNet`.
- Define ControlNet as the specific neural-network architecture introduced by Zhang, Rao, and Agrawala for adding learned spatial/structural conditioning to a large pretrained diffusion model while preserving the pretrained backbone and injecting learned control features into its denoising network.
- Present the original architecture accurately: a pretrained network backbone is kept locked, trainable copies of relevant encoding blocks learn the added condition, and zero-initialized convolutional connections inject the learned features so the added branch starts with minimal perturbation of the pretrained model.
- Treat the locked/trainable-copy and zero-initialized-connection design as the defining original ControlNet formulation rather than requiring every later derivative or compatible implementation to reproduce every block, layer count, or connection exactly.
- Distinguish ControlNet from generic diffusion conditioning. Text embeddings, class labels, masks, image conditioning, classifier-free guidance, cross-attention conditioning, adapters, reference-image features, or other control mechanisms are not automatically ControlNet.
- Distinguish ControlNet from a concrete ControlNet checkpoint/model artifact. The architecture is reusable; concrete checkpoints are trained for particular base-model families, condition representations, datasets, resolutions, or objectives and retain their catalog/model ownership.
- Distinguish the ControlNet network/checkpoint from the condition preprocessor. An edge detector, depth estimator, pose detector, segmentation model, normal estimator, line-art extractor, scribble processor, or other preprocessing stage creates or transforms the conditioning signal but is not itself ControlNet.
- Distinguish the condition representation from ground truth. A detected depth/pose/edge/segmentation map can be noisy, ambiguous, incomplete, biased, or estimated from another model; ControlNet follows the supplied learned condition rather than certifying its correctness.
- Explain common spatial condition families as examples rather than a closed list: edges, depth, pose, segmentation, normals, line art, scribbles, keypoints, layouts, or other structured maps can be used when a compatible ControlNet has been trained for that representation.
- Do not define ControlNet as image-to-image generation. A ControlNet-conditioned diffusion pipeline can use a condition derived from an image while the generative operation can still be text-to-image or another conditioned diffusion task; `image-to-image/` owns transformation where an existing image itself is the source image state.
- Do not define ControlNet as prompt guidance. Text prompts and ControlNet conditions can interact, but prompt conditioning controls semantic/content instructions while the ControlNet branch provides an additional learned structural/spatial constraint according to its training.
- Explain base-model compatibility explicitly. A ControlNet checkpoint is coupled to assumptions about the pretrained diffusion architecture, feature shapes/channels, conditioning interfaces, latent/data resolution, and often a model family/version; a checkpoint trained for one backbone is not automatically compatible with another.
- Explain representation compatibility separately from visual similarity. Two control images can look similar while using different encodings/ranges/channels/preprocessing conventions; the control checkpoint must receive the representation it was trained to interpret.
- Explain training at the architecture level. The original method freezes the production diffusion backbone and trains the added control branch so conditioning can be learned without directly overwriting the locked base weights; concrete objectives, datasets, optimizers, schedules, and training recipes remain implementation/evidence material.
- Explain zero convolutions accurately as zero-initialized learned connections in the original architecture. They provide zero output initially and can learn non-zero influence through training; do not describe them as permanently zero, non-trainable, or a general property of every diffusion control method.
- Do not claim the original frozen-backbone design guarantees absence of catastrophic forgetting, overfitting, or quality regression in every derivative/training setup. It protects the locked parameters but final behavior still depends on the learned control branch, data, optimization, base model, and inference integration.
- Explain control influence as continuous/configurable at inference only where the concrete implementation supports it. Control weight/scale, start/end fractions, per-block strengths, or schedules are runtime/checkpoint interfaces rather than universal fields of the abstract architecture.
- Do not define a universal `control strength` scale. A numeric value has meaning only under a specific runtime, checkpoint, scheduler/sampler, condition normalization, and pipeline implementation.
- Explain prompt-control conflict as a system behavior rather than a deterministic rule. When text/model priors and structural conditions disagree, outputs can prioritize, compromise, distort, or ignore parts of either signal depending on training, strength, denoising stage, and model capacity.
- Explain multi-ControlNet/multiple-condition composition as an extension/use pattern rather than a requirement. Systems can combine multiple compatible control branches, but interactions can be non-additive and require empirical evaluation.
- Distinguish ControlNet from T2I-Adapter, IP-Adapter, reference-attention methods, inpainting masks, LoRA, and other adaptation/conditioning families. Similar user-visible control goals do not imply the same architecture or interchangeable checkpoints.
- Distinguish ControlNet from LoRA/PEFT. ControlNet adds a conditioning branch/architecture around a diffusion backbone; LoRA is a low-rank parameter-adaptation method. A concrete system can use both, but they solve different adaptation/control problems.
- Distinguish ControlNet from latent space itself. A ControlNet can inject features into a latent-diffusion U-Net or another compatible diffusion network, while `latent-space/` owns the generic latent-representation concept.
- Explain that ControlNet is not inherently limited to one specific condition source, but the original method and most concrete checkpoints learn a defined mapping from a condition representation to control features. Arbitrary inputs are not meaningful without compatible training/preprocessing.
- Explain that the architecture does not guarantee exact geometric constraint satisfaction. Diffusion generation remains learned/stochastic and can deviate from edges, pose, depth, segmentation, counts, identity, proportions, text, or other requested structure.
- Explain preprocessing error propagation. Incorrect pose joints, missing edges, bad depth discontinuities, segmentation mistakes, scaling/cropping mismatch, or coordinate transforms can become misleading control signals and should be validated when spatial fidelity matters.
- Explain spatial alignment requirements. Resize, crop, aspect-ratio changes, padding, coordinate conventions, and augmentation can misalign condition maps with the generated canvas/latent representation; pipelines must preserve the checkpoint's expected alignment contract.
- Explain that output quality/control fidelity are separate dimensions. Strong structural adherence can coexist with poor visual quality or semantic mismatch, and visually strong output can ignore the intended condition; evaluation should measure the actual target property rather than one aesthetic score.
- Explain base-model capability dependence. ControlNet adds control to a pretrained diffusion model but does not create subjects, styles, text-rendering ability, identities, knowledge, or safety capabilities absent from the base model/training ecosystem.
- Explain computational/resource impact as implementation-dependent. Added control branches/processors increase parameters, memory traffic, preprocessing cost, and/or inference compute to varying degrees; do not publish one universal overhead percentage.
- Explain checkpoint stacking/merging carefully. ControlNet checkpoints, base model modifications, LoRAs, VAEs, schedulers, and other adapters can interact; compatibility and result quality must be evaluated for the concrete pipeline rather than inferred from individually compatible components.
- Explain model/version lineage. A derived or converted ControlNet can change architecture details, tensor formats, condition encoders, precision, quantization, or base-model assumptions; naming alone is not proof that it implements the original method or is compatible with another pipeline.
- Keep concrete ControlNet checkpoints, variants/derivatives, preprocessors, base models, model files, hashes, tensor shapes, conditioning-channel formats, runtime nodes/APIs, supported resolutions, control weights/schedules, benchmark outcomes, compatibility matrices, and workflow recipes with their applicable catalog/evidence/learning/project owners.
- Use the canonical entity references as research inputs for ControlNet identity and the original spatial-conditioning architecture when reader-facing rendering is activated.

## Validation

- ControlNet is defined as the specific architecture/method introduced by the original work, not as a generic label for all diffusion control or conditioning.
- The original locked/trainable-copy and zero-initialized-connection design is described accurately without forcing every derivative to be bit-for-bit identical.
- ControlNet architecture, concrete checkpoints, condition preprocessors, and condition maps remain distinct entities/concepts.
- Edge/depth/pose/segmentation examples are not treated as guaranteed ground truth or an exhaustive control taxonomy.
- Base-model and condition-representation compatibility are explicit and are not inferred from filenames or matching image dimensions alone.
- Control strength, multi-control composition, scheduler settings, runtime nodes, and workflow recipes are not universalized as part of the architecture definition.
- ControlNet is not claimed to guarantee exact spatial constraint satisfaction, quality, safety, or preservation of every base-model capability.
- Concrete models/checkpoints, preprocessors, formats, compatibility facts, benchmarks, and workflow guidance remain outside the reusable ControlNet concept.
