# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Use the reader-facing title `Machine Learning (ML)`.
- Define machine learning as a family of computational methods in which models or decision functions are learned or adapted from data or experience rather than specified entirely through fixed manually authored rules.
- Present machine learning as an important approach within artificial intelligence, while making clear that not every AI system uses machine learning and not every AI behavior is explained by ML alone.
- Explain the distinction between a learning/training process and the resulting fitted model or policy used for prediction, generation, decision support, or action.
- Explain generalization as the central requirement that learned behavior must be evaluated beyond the examples or interactions used to fit it; training fit alone is not evidence of useful out-of-sample performance.
- Avoid defining ML as necessarily gradient-based, neural-network-based, or parameterized in one specific way; algorithms and learning paradigms vary.
- Orient readers toward the selected learning-paradigm, representation-learning, data-centric, probabilistic/causal, optimization/AutoML, and learning-theory subdomains without duplicating their canonical content.
- Keep concrete datasets, model artifacts, benchmark results, product recommendations, deployment procedures, and monitoring recipes with their applicable catalog, evidence, decision, or engineering owners.
- Render the standard direct-child navigation from the validated materialized child set when reader-facing rendering is activated.
- Use the canonical entity references as research inputs for baseline ML terminology.

## Validation

- The page does not equate machine learning with deep learning or neural networks alone.
- The page does not claim that fitting historical/training data demonstrates generalization.
- The page does not require one particular learning algorithm, objective, optimizer, or parameter-update mechanism as part of the definition.
- The page distinguishes the learning process from inference/use of the resulting model.
- Direct-child navigation contains only currently materialized direct children and does not imply full taxonomy materialization.
- Operational guidance from the legacy source is not duplicated as canonical ML definition content.
