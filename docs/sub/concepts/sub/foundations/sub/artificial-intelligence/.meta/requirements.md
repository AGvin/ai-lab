# Documentation Requirements

## Requirements

- Use the reader-facing title `Artificial Intelligence (AI)`.
- Present artificial intelligence as a broad field and umbrella for machine-based systems and methods that produce capabilities such as prediction, content generation, recommendation, decision support, perception, language processing, reasoning, planning, or action.
- Distinguish the broad field/concept of AI from the narrower term `AI system`. When discussing systems, use current authoritative terminology that emphasizes machine-based systems inferring how to generate outputs from inputs for explicit or implicit objectives, with varying autonomy and adaptiveness.
- Make clear that AI is not synonymous with machine learning, deep learning, generative AI, large language models, or autonomous agents; these are narrower methods, model classes, or system patterns within or related to the broader field.
- Acknowledge that AI systems can combine learned models with search, planning, symbolic or probabilistic reasoning, deterministic rules, tools, retrieval, and human controls rather than assuming one implementation mechanism.
- Distinguish a model from the broader AI system in which it may operate; model capability alone does not describe surrounding software, tools, data flows, controls, users, or operational context.
- Avoid treating task performance, fluent generation, or the `AI` label as evidence of general intelligence, human-like understanding, autonomy, reliability, or suitability for unrelated tasks.
- Keep product-selection advice, current model comparisons, implementation recipes, and policy-specific legal definitions outside this foundational concept unless they are needed only to clarify terminology boundaries.
- Use the canonical entity references as research inputs for current AI-system terminology when reader-facing rendering is activated.

## Validation

- The page does not reduce AI to LLMs, generative models, or machine learning alone.
- The page distinguishes AI models from complete AI systems and does not infer system behavior solely from a model label.
- Capabilities are described without implying universal autonomy, general intelligence, human-equivalent cognition, or reliability.
- Regulatory or standards definitions are identified as scoped definitions when used and are not presented as the only possible description of the entire academic/engineering field.
- The page remains conceptual and does not duplicate concrete ecosystem facts or model-selection guidance.
