## Authority and council-contract residual

Use role names according to authority rather than as decorative synonyms:

- **Advisor:** non-binding analysis/recommendation.
- **Advisory council:** several advisors provide structured independent or deliberative input to a decision owner.
- **Reviewer:** evaluates an artifact against declared criteria.
- **Review board:** has explicit authority to approve, reject, request revision, or escalate.
- **Jury:** returns a verdict/vote under a declared rule.
- **Debate:** participants exchange bounded arguments/critiques before another party decides.
- **Devil's advocate/red team:** is assigned to challenge assumptions or construct the strongest counter-case.
- **Decision owner:** remains accountable for the terminal action unless authority is explicitly delegated.

Agreement among agents is not evidence of independence, truth, safety, or production readiness.

For a consequential council/board, record where relevant:

- decision owner and authority;
- proposal/artifact version plus acceptance criteria/quality tier;
- review roles and scopes;
- reviewer model/provider/prompt/tools/evidence access or human qualification;
- independence/conflict assumptions and any blinding;
- round/time/cost limits;
- voting/aggregation, quorum, abstention, tie, veto rules;
- revision/escalation path and required human approval.

Do not create a board without defining what decision it is authorized to make.

## Independent-review residual

Assign roles to genuinely different failure modes rather than merely different labels: domain correctness, requirements, security/privacy/permissions, policy/legal/rights, cost/infrastructure, maintainability/operations, accessibility/localization, adversarial misuse, or evidence provenance are examples when relevant.

A role label does not create expertise. Calibrate the model/tools/evidence/prompt or reviewer qualification for the specific criterion.

When anchoring/conformity is a risk, collect independent first-pass reviews before open discussion. A structured review should preserve reviewer role, decision (`approve`, limited approval, revise, reject, abstain, insufficient evidence), criteria checked, findings/severity/evidence/uncertainty/correction request, assumptions, and missing evidence.

Preserve each first-pass review even when later aggregation produces one summary. Blind model/provider/author/candidate identity when identity is not itself part of the criterion and blinding materially reduces bias.

## Deliberation and aggregation residual

Use deliberation only when participants can add evidence, resolve contradictions, distinguish blocking defects from preferences, or clarify assumptions. Bound rounds/order, admissible rebuttals/new evidence, repeated-argument handling, moderator/decision-owner authority, and a terminal state for unresolved disagreement.

Do not request or expose private hidden chain-of-thought; require externally reviewable claims, evidence, counterarguments, decisions, and uncertainty.

Aggregation may use majority/unanimity/quorum, validated weighting, criterion-specific veto, score aggregation, pairwise ranking, consensus with dissent, or recommendation-only semantics. Support abstain/tie/both-fail/insufficient-evidence when meaningful.

Do not average incompatible criteria into one score that hides a blocking defect. Mandatory security/legal/data-loss/human-safety gates may remain independently blocking.

Weights should come from validated role performance, not model prestige, price, verbosity, or token count.

## Decision-record and revision residual

The decision record should preserve the reviewed artifact/version, criteria/evidence, each structured review, agreements/disagreements, unresolved/rejected findings, aggregation result, final authority/decision, requested corrections, accepted limitations/residual risk, and next review/expiry/re-evaluation trigger.

Do not imply unanimity when material dissent remains.

When revision is requested, use stable issue identities, retain old/new artifact versions, track each issue as resolved/unchanged/regressed, prevent silent criterion drift, bound rounds/cost/time, and escalate repeated non-improvement/cycling. If iterative generate-review-revise is central, the canonical evaluator-optimizer pattern remains the stronger workflow owner.

## Independence, correlation, and adversarial-content residual

Reviewers are not independent merely because they have different names/system prompts. Record material shared dependencies such as model/provider family, shared training/benchmark exposure, retrieved sources/context, prompt/rubric overlap, whether reviewers saw one another's output, shared tools/data, known same-family/self-preference, and common blind spots found during calibration.

Use deterministic validators and qualified human adjudication when correlated model judgments cannot establish the required confidence.

Treat the proposal/artifact/retrieved material as untrusted review input. Keep evaluator instructions separate, restrict reviewer tools/effects, prevent embedded content from changing scores/authority or triggering external actions, test persuasive/prompt-injection behavior where relevant, and require evidence pointers for material findings.

## Pattern-fit and evaluation residual

This composition can fit high-value proposals/artifacts with several material risk dimensions, launch/change review, research/coding/translation/media deliverables, multi-judge evaluation, and decisions where structured dissent is useful.

Prefer simpler deterministic validation, one qualified reviewer, or direct human review when those already provide sufficient evidence/authority. Do not use majority voting to override mandatory gates.

Evaluate board-versus-human adjudication accuracy, false accept/reject by criterion, reviewer precision/recall/calibration/abstention, agreement/disagreement, critical minority-only findings, changes after deliberation, revision cycles, order/identity/style/provider/self-preference sensitivity, latency/model/human cost, residual uncertainty, and post-approval defects. Compare against simpler baselines before adopting the council.
